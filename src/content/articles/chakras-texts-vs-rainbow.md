Search for "chakras" and you'll find the same picture everywhere: seven coloured circles up the spine, red at the base, then orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet at the crown. It's tidy, easy to remember and printed on everything from yoga mats to candles.

It's also not what the tradition describes. The rainbow chart is a 20th-century Western creation. The Sanskrit texts it claims to summarise paint a stranger and much richer picture.

## Where the chakras come from

*Cakra* means "wheel". The texts also call these centres *padma*, "lotus", and that's how they are always drawn: lotuses along a central channel, the *suṣumnā*, running up through the spine.

Chakras belong to the **tantric** traditions, Hindu and Buddhist, which flourished from roughly the 7th century onward. They aren't in Patanjali's *Yoga Sutras*, and they aren't in the early Upanishads in their later form. Different tantric texts list different numbers: some describe four, some five, nine, twelve or more. Tibetan Buddhist systems usually work with four or five.

The seven-centre system everyone knows comes mostly from one text: the **Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa**, "the description of the six chakras", written by the Bengali teacher Pūrṇānanda in the 16th century as a chapter of a larger work. In 1919 Sir John Woodroffe, writing as Arthur Avalon, translated it into English as *The Serpent Power*. Almost every modern book on chakras traces back to that translation.

Notice the title: **six** chakras. The thousand-petalled lotus at the crown, the Sahasrāra, isn't counted as one of them. It sits above, the place where the journey ends.

## What the text actually describes

Each lotus in the *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa* is a whole meditation image, not a coloured dot. Each has:

- **A number of petals**, each carrying a Sanskrit letter
- **An element** (*tattva*) drawn as a geometric shape
- **A seed syllable** (*bīja mantra*)
- **A deity and a goddess**, sometimes an animal, each with their own colour

| Chakra | Where | Petals | Element and shape | Seed syllable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mūlādhāra | base of the spine | 4 | earth, a square | लं *laṃ* |
| Svādhiṣṭhāna | at the root of the genitals | 6 | water, a crescent moon | वं *vaṃ* |
| Maṇipūra | navel | 10 | fire, a downward triangle | रं *raṃ* |
| Anāhata | heart | 12 | air, a six-pointed star | यं *yaṃ* |
| Viśuddha | throat | 16 | space, a full moon in a triangle | हं *haṃ* |
| Ājñā | between the eyebrows | 2 | the mind | ॐ *oṃ* |
| Sahasrāra | above the head | 1,000 | beyond the elements | — |

## The colours: not a rainbow

The text does give colours, and lots of them, but they don't line up into a spectrum. The four petals of the Mūlādhāra are **red**. The twelve petals of the Anāhata, the heart, are described as **vermilion**: also red, not green. And the thousand-petalled lotus at the crown is the clearest case of all:

<div class="aside">

In Avalon's translation, the Sahasrāra is **"whiter than the full moon"**. Not violet. Not purple. White: the colour of pure light, of the moon over the Himalaya.

</div>

The element regions, deities and seed syllables each add their own colours on top: a yellow seed syllable here, a smoky region there, a lightning-bright goddess in another. The point of all this detail was never a colour code. It was a precise image to hold in meditation, one lotus after another, as attention rises along the spine.

## Where the rainbow came from

The rainbow chart has a traceable history. Historian Kurt Leland, in *Rainbow Body* (2016), follows it through Western esoteric writing:

- **Theosophy (late 1800s to 1920s).** Writers such as C. W. Leadbeater described chakras from their own "clairvoyant" investigations, with their own colours and even their own locations, including a chakra at the spleen.
- **Colour healing (1920s to 1950s).** Western colour therapists, notably Ivah Bergh Whitten and her student Roland Hunt, matched colours to the body's centres.
- **The 1970s.** Ken Dychtwald's *Bodymind* (1977) put the seven rainbow colours together with the endocrine glands and a list of psychological qualities for each chakra. That package, rainbow plus glands plus "root chakra = safety", is the chart most people know today. Writers such as Anodea Judith later built it into a full psychological system.

None of this makes the modern chart useless. Many people find it a helpful map. But it's a modern Western map, and calling it "ancient" hides the real source.

## Why the difference matters

The rainbow chart turns the chakras into a self-help tool: unblock your throat chakra to speak your truth, balance your sacral chakra for creativity. The traditional system has a different aim. In the *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa*, the lotuses are stages in **kuṇḍalinī** practice: the awakened power rises from the Mūlādhāra, dissolving each element into the next, earth into water, water into fire, fire into air, air into space, space into mind, until it reaches the Sahasrāra and merges with pure consciousness. The text says that one who knows this is not born again.

It's the same direction as the rest of yoga: from the gross to the subtle, from the body to the seer. The chakras are a ladder, not a colour wheel.
