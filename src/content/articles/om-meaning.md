You hear it at the start and end of yoga classes, see it printed on mats and tattoos, and hear it at every temple in Nepal. Om is probably the best-known sound in the spiritual world. But in the tradition, it isn't a decorative opening. It's a complete practice in itself.

## The sound and the symbol

Om is written in Devanagari as **ॐ** and pronounced as one long syllable. Because in Sanskrit the vowel *o* is formed from *a* and *u* blended together, it's also written **AUM**: three sounds flowing into one.

It is sacred across Hindu, Buddhist and Jain traditions. Mantras usually begin with it. In Nepal you'll hear it in temple bells, in the Buddhist *Om maṇi padme hūṃ* turning on prayer wheels, and in household prayers every morning.

## What the Upanishads say

The short *Mandukya Upanishad* is devoted entirely to Om. It maps the syllable onto the states of consciousness:

- **A:** the waking state, awareness turned outward
- **U:** the dreaming state, awareness turned inward
- **M:** deep, dreamless sleep, where awareness rests undivided
- **The silence after:** the fourth (*turīya*), pure awareness that underlies all three

That last point is easy to miss. In this teaching, **the silence after Om matters as much as the sound.** The chant is a way of moving through the layers of the mind to the stillness behind them.

## What the Yoga Sutras say

Patanjali brings Om into yoga directly, in the first chapter:

<div class="verse">
<p class="dev" lang="sa">तस्य वाचकः प्रणवः</p>
<p class="iast">tasya vācakaḥ praṇavaḥ</p>
<p class="tr">The word that expresses it [Īśvara, the divine] is Om (praṇava).</p>
<p class="ref">Yoga Sutras 1.27</p>
</div>

<div class="verse">
<p class="dev" lang="sa">तज्जपस्तदर्थभावनम्</p>
<p class="iast">taj-japas tad-artha-bhāvanam</p>
<p class="tr">Repeat it, and dwell on its meaning.</p>
<p class="ref">Yoga Sutras 1.28</p>
</div>

The next sutra says that from this practice the mind turns inward and obstacles fall away. In other words, chanting Om is offered as a path to the still mind that is yoga's goal, not just a way to start a class.

## How to chant Om as a practice

1. **Sit upright**, comfortably, with the spine long.
2. **Take a full, unhurried breath.**
3. **Begin with "ah"**, open, from the back of the throat.
4. **Let it round into "oo"**, the sound moving forward in the mouth.
5. **Close the lips into "mm"**, and feel the hum in the head and chest.
6. **Rest in the silence** afterwards for as long as the last one took, or longer.
7. **Repeat** three times, or for a few minutes, keeping attention on the sound and its meaning.

<div class="aside">

**A common mistake** is to rush through Om as a group warm-up and move straight on. Try giving the silence at the end its full time. That pause is where the practice works.

</div>

## Respecting what it is

Om is sacred to hundreds of millions of people. Using it in practice, sincerely, is welcome in the tradition, which has always shared it widely. What jars is using it as decoration: on flip-flops, bathmats or anything that touches the floor. A small rule many people here follow: keep Om at the level of the heart or above.
