  - q: "What is Maya in Vedanta?"
    a: "The power of Brahman that makes the one reality appear as many: as this universe, these bodies and these minds. It has two powers: the veiling power (āvaraṇa śakti), which hides the reality, and the projecting power (vikṣepa śakti), which presents it as something else."
  - q: "What is the rope and snake example?"
    a: "In dim light you mistake a rope for a snake. Two things happen: you don't see the rope (veiling) and you see a snake instead (projecting). Advaita Vedanta says the same happens with Brahman and the world. The snake is removed only by knowing the rope, not by fighting the snake."
  - q: "Does Maya mean the world is not real?"
    a: "Not that it doesn't appear. Vedanta says the world is real as an appearance, with a borrowed reality, the way a wave is real as a form of water. What is unreal is its independence from Brahman."
  - q: "Is Maya the cause of suffering?"
    a: "According to the Drg-Drsya Viveka, the veiling power is. The projecting power alone is no problem: an enlightened person still sees the world. The trouble begins when the difference between the seer and the seen, and between Brahman and the world, is hidden."

[Part 1](/the-texts/seer-and-the-seen/) found the witness within. [Part 2](/the-texts/mirror-of-the-mind/) showed how that witness gets reflected in the mind. Now the text turns outward and asks the bigger question: if there is only Brahman, one existence-consciousness-bliss, where do this mind, this body and this whole universe come from?

Ask any Advaita teacher that question and you will hear one word. **Māyā.**

## The power of Brahman

Maya is the power in Brahman that projects Brahman itself as the universe. It is neither a second reality next to Brahman (that would break non-duality) nor simply identical with it (then nothing would ever appear). The traditional image is fire and its power to burn. They aren't two things, yet the fire isn't just its burning.

<div class="verse">
<p class="dev" lang="sa">शक्तिद्वयं हि मायाया विक्षेपावृतिरूपकम् ।<br>विक्षेपशक्तिर्लिङ्गादि ब्रह्माण्डान्तं जगत्सृजेत् ॥</p>
<p class="iast">śakti-dvayaṃ hi māyāyā vikṣepāvṛti-rūpakam<br>vikṣepa-śaktir liṅgādi brahmāṇḍāntaṃ jagat sṛjet</p>
<p class="tr">Maya has two powers: projecting and veiling. The projecting power creates everything, from the subtle body to the whole cosmos.</p>
<p class="ref">Dṛg-Dṛśya Viveka 13</p>
</div>

## The rope and the snake

This sounds like metaphysics, but Swami Sarvapriyananda points out that it's what happens in every ordinary mistake. Walk into a dim room and see a coiled snake. Later you find it's a rope. Two things happened:

1. **You didn't see the rope.** That's the **veiling power**, *āvaraṇa śakti*.
2. **You saw a snake instead.** That's the **projecting power**, *vikṣepa śakti*.

Advaita says exactly this is going on with the universe. The one reality is hidden, and in its place we see a world of separate things and separate selves. And notice: while you were seeing the snake, where was the rope? Right there. You were looking straight at it. The length and shape of the "snake" were the rope's. Only the poison and the fear were added.

## Name and form, like foam on the ocean

How does the projecting power make a universe? Verse 14 answers in a single line:

<div class="verse">
<p class="dev" lang="sa">सृष्टिर्नाम ब्रह्मरूपे सच्चिदानन्दवस्तुनि ।<br>अब्धौ फेनादिवत्सर्वनामरूपप्रसारणा ॥</p>
<p class="iast">sṛṣṭir nāma brahma-rūpe sac-cid-ānanda-vastuni<br>abdhau phenādivat sarva-nāma-rūpa-prasāraṇā</p>
<p class="tr">Creation is the spreading of names and forms over Brahman, existence-consciousness-bliss, like foam on the ocean.</p>
<p class="ref">Dṛg-Dṛśya Viveka 14</p>
</div>

Maya casts a net of **name and form** (*nāma-rūpa*) over Brahman. We take a table: wood with a shape and a word. We take a wave: water with a shape and a word. We know wood and water, so we can see through the table and the wave whenever we like. What we don't know is Brahman. So we see only the names and forms, and never what they're made of.

The *Aṣṭāvakra Gītā* takes the image of the ocean three steps deeper. First: I am an infinite ocean, and the universe is a little boat drifting on me. Then: it isn't a boat, it's a wave, my own movement; let it rise and fall, I gain and lose nothing. Then, deepest: there is no wave at all. The universe is only imagined in me, as the whole ocean appears in a mirror without a drop of water in it.

## The projecting power is not the problem

Here the text makes a surprising point. The projection isn't what makes us suffer. Swami Sarvapriyananda uses three examples:

- **The mirage.** Once you know it's a mirage, you still see water when you look back across the desert. But you don't run to it.
- **The blue sky.** Physics tells you the sky has no blue paint. You still see it blue, and you don't wash the blue off the planes.
- **The cinema.** Hollywood, he notes, is where the projecting power is at its peak: disasters, murders and alien invasions on every screen. We don't panic. We give them Oscars. We enjoy the projection because we know the screen.

The enlightened person still sees the world. The difference is that they know what it is. So they can enjoy the play, and, the swami stresses, they don't shrug at other people's suffering because "it's only a movie". They go and help.

## The veiling power is

<div class="verse">
<p class="dev" lang="sa">अन्तर्दृग्दृश्ययोर्भेदं बहिश्च ब्रह्मसर्गयोः ।<br>आवृणोत्यपरा शक्तिः सा संसारस्य कारणम् ॥</p>
<p class="iast">antar dṛg-dṛśyayor bhedaṃ bahiś ca brahma-sargayoḥ<br>āvṛṇoty aparā śaktiḥ sā saṃsārasya kāraṇam</p>
<p class="tr">Inside, the difference between the seer and the seen; outside, the difference between Brahman and creation: the other power veils both. That is the cause of saṃsāra.</p>
<p class="ref">Dṛg-Dṛśya Viveka 15</p>
</div>

Read it carefully. Maya doesn't hide Brahman. It can't: Brahman is the very existence of everything you see. What it hides is the **difference**: inside, between the witness and the mind; outside, between Brahman and the world. They come to us mixed up, like a movie that completely covers its screen.

And then the chain runs. Taking myself to be a limited body and mind, I feel incomplete. Feeling incomplete, I want. Wanting, I act, and become the doer (*kartā*). Acting, I get results, and become the experiencer (*bhoktā*) of pleasure and, mostly, pain. Results take more than one life to play out, so lives follow lives. That's *saṃsāra*.

## Who are you?

Swami Sarvapriyananda tells of a devotee who kept pressing Ramakrishna: why is there so much suffering? Ramakrishna gave one answer after another. It is God's will. It is God's play, his *līlā*. The devotee burst out: it may be play for him, but it's death for us! Ramakrishna replied with two words: *who are you?*

A wave fighting other waves sees tragedy. The ocean sees its own play. And the wave is the ocean.

## Why does Maya exist at all?

Everyone asks. The swami's answer is sharp: Maya is space, time and causation. To ask "why?" is to ask for a cause. So the question already stands inside Maya, and can't be answered from there. Vedanta doesn't solve that question. It dissolves the one who needs it answered.

## What this means for yoga

Patanjali's yoga, built on Sāṅkhya, treats the seen world as real and seeks realisation by separating the seer from it, as if switching off the film. Advaita, says Swami Sarvapriyananda, doesn't ask you to switch it off. It asks you to recognise the screen. The famous summary, traditionally attributed to Shankara, puts the whole of this section in a line:

<div class="verse">
<p class="dev" lang="sa">ब्रह्म सत्यं जगन्मिथ्या जीवो ब्रह्मैव नापरः</p>
<p class="iast">brahma satyaṃ jagan mithyā jīvo brahmaiva nāparaḥ</p>
<p class="tr">Brahman is real, the world is appearance, and the individual is Brahman alone, not other.</p>
</div>
